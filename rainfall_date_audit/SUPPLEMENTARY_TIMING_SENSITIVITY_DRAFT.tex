\subsection{Rainfall date-label provenance sensitivity (supplementary diagnostic)}
To characterize temporal provenance uncertainty, we compared the authors'
processed stationwise daily precipitation labels with a separately published,
processed BMD-derived station dataset
(\url{https://doi.org/10.17632/tbrhznpwg9.1}) and limited available
BAMIS archived daily reports. The public compilation is not a certified
original BMD raw export, and may contain interpolated or otherwise processed
values. We therefore treat agreement as evidence of a possible difference
in calendar labels, \emph{not} verification of physical precipitation
observation intervals.

Source-quality diagnostics found that the public Bogra 2021 station-year
repeated its 2020 rainfall values on 365/365 comparable dates; the public
Teknaf 2023 station-year repeated 2022 values on 332/365 dates. These
station-years were excluded from the sensitivity candidate and the
corresponding three-way frozen-prediction comparison. The authors'
Ambagan 2021 rainfall had no available numeric values for alignment.
Across the remaining comparable station-years, author-$D-1$ agreement
reached 96.99\% in 2021 and 95.83\% in 2023. A proposed independent
rainfall-only date-label scenario was generated for 67 station-years,
reassigning the author observation at $D-1$ to hypothetical target
date $D$ without copying public-source rainfall values. This is only
a sensitivity scenario. It left 418 candidate values missing and did not
change the primary BMD inputs or historical predictor series.

To inspect robustness using previously trained models, we scored 34 saved
leave-one-station-out precipitation prediction folds under three observation
sequences (original author labels, hypothetical realignment, and separately
processed public source), using identical station/date supports for all three
scenarios. For example, raw ERA5-Land rainfall RMSE in 2021 was 13.949\,mm
against original labels and 15.786\,mm against the aligned candidate
($n=11{,}095$ days); the 2023 values were 15.233 and 17.663\,mm
($n=11{,}325$ days), respectively. This deterioration neither proves the
original calendar labels correct nor invalidates the independent source
comparison: existing models and contemporaneous predictor dates were
\emph{not retrained or relabelled}. These re-scorings must not be interpreted
as new model training or corrected-validation results.

The original BMD rainfall day-reporting convention, station-specific historical
site positions, raw-source corrections, and some third-party preprocessing
remain unverified. The existing model-performance claims therefore remain
conditional on the supplied processed observations, and a source-authorized
correction would require consistently rebuilding training and validation
folds before reporting corrected performance. No shift was selected on the
basis of improved downstream model test scores.
